a social media strategy for Paper Boat

Every generation had a paper boat.

Paper Boat built its name on 90s nostalgia. The next generation of buyers grew up in the 2000s and 2010s. This strategy makes Paper Boat the home of everyone's childhood memories, and turns followers into the people telling them.

What's working ✅Distinctive illustrated look, pouches people recognise, famous films like "Rizwan", and a single clear idea: drinks and memories.
What's missing 🟡Big campaigns come and go; there's no always-on content engine or repeatable formats followers expect every week.
The risk 🔴90s nostalgia speaks to millennials. Gen Z's childhood was different: cartoons, power cuts, tiffin swaps, the 2010s. If the memories don't match, the brand ages with its audience.
Chapter 2

the strategy on one page

Let followers write the memories.

Paper Boat has always told memories. Now it collects them. Every generation's childhood becomes content, and every follower becomes a storyteller.

GoalGrow Instagram from ~200K to 300K followers in 12 months, with engagement driven by saves and shares, not giveaways.
AudienceCore: 22–35, city-based, nostalgic snackers. Growth: 18–24 Gen Z discovering the brand through their own memories.
Platform rolesInstagram for the memory engine; YouTube Shorts for reach; LinkedIn for brand and hiring; WhatsApp Channel for new flavours and drops.
VoiceWarm, lowercase, a little cheeky. Talks like an old friend who remembers everything.
Rhythm5 Reels, 2 carousels and daily Stories a week; one creator collab a fortnight.

House rules

  • Reply to every comment within the first hour after posting.
  • Ask a question back; memories grow in the replies.
  • Price or quality complaints go to DMs the same day.
  • Always ask before reposting someone's memory.
  • No "90s kids only" gatekeeping: every generation's memories count.
