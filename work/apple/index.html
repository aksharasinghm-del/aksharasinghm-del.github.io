the problem (read me first)
Chapter 1 of 12

Apple doesn't have a customer problem. It has a meaning problem. The iPhone is becoming the villain in Gen Z's mental health story.

the receipts

apple's answer so far

More Screen Time dashboards and parental controls. Useful, but they're settings for parents. They don't give a 21-year-old one reason to love Apple more.

brand audit

strengths

weaknesses

  • wellbeing messaging is defensive
  • stores are for buying, not belonging

opportunities

  • Gen Z wants real-life community: run clubs, phone-free events
  • no big tech brand owns "logging off"

threats

  • school bans and age-check laws keep spreading
  • dumbphones make "less Apple" a lifestyle

The insight

Logging off is easy. Having somewhere to go is the hard part.

Gen Z doesn't need another screen-time timer. They need a better place to be when the phone goes down. Apple already owns hundreds of those places.

The idea
Chapter 5
Introducing
Apple Offline.
Apple's first product you don't hold.
In one line
Free weekly clubs in Apple Stores, plus an Offline Mode on iPhone.
So Gen Z can put the phone down without missing out.
Part 1: the feature
Offline Mode
One swipe. Social apps pause, notifications stop, but Maps, Music, Camera and Pay keep working. It counts time offline, not time on screen.
Part 2: the new vertical
Offline Clubs
Apple Stores become Gen Z's new hangout after hours. Run Club, Listening Club, Photo Walk, Make Club. Free, with Offline Mode on at the door.
Part 3: the share moment
Offline Wrapped
Every December: your hours offline, top club and people you met. Built to be shared. Yes, we know the irony.
Why only Apple
It owns the software, the stores, the Watch and the culture.
Nobody else can switch off the phone and open a door at the same time.
Where the money is
Leave the phone. Keep the Watch.
Going offline safely is easier with Apple Watch and AirPods. Clubs are free, and the ecosystem gets more useful.

Chapter 7: the new vertical

Free clubs in Apple Stores, after hours. Pick a city and RSVP.

Launching in 10 cities, then expanding based on return rates. Partner cafés, bookshops and parks host clubs where there's no store.

Chapter 9: the social media plan

A log-off campaign that lives online. We say the irony out loud, and use social to get people offline, not to keep them scrolling.

TikTokJoin the screen-time and dumbphone conversation honestly.Creator "24 hours offline" videos, club recaps. KPI: shares, sign-ups.
InstagramThe club home: weekly drops and member stories.Stories with RSVP stickers, carousels, city Broadcast channels. KPI: RSVPs.
YouTubeThe launch film and "We met at Run Club" series.60-second film, 8-minute episodes, Shorts. KPI: watch time, brand lift.
StravaRun Club lives where runners already are.Official club, route challenges. KPI: members, event joins.
Threads and XLaunch-day conversation around the apple.com stunt.Real-time replies. KPI: mentions, earned media.
Content pillars
Club life 35%Creators log off 25%Club drops 25%How-to 15%
Creators

Big creators go offline for 24 hours on launch. Run-club founders, book-club hosts and DJs lead the first clubs. Local creators film their city. Members, with consent, become the main content.

Paid

Tease to 18–26 in launch cities, then "Find a club" creator ads, then local club-drop Stories to people within 10 km of a store.
